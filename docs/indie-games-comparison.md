# Cross-Genre Case Study Comparison: Independent Games and Their Design Lessons

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document compares five independent-game case studies: *Minecraft*, *Undertale*, *The Binding of Isaac*, *Stardew Valley*, and *Hollow Knight*. They were selected purposively to represent different design and production patterns: player-created worlds, character-centred narrative, procedural replayability, solo-developed life simulation, and small-team world design. This is a qualitative desk-based comparison, not a representative sample of all indie games and not a ranking of which game is best.

The comparison uses eight criteria consistently across all five cases. Descriptions of game mechanics are treated separately from interpretations of their cultural or industrial significance. The term **independent game** is not assumed to have one universal definition; funding, ownership, creative control, team size, and distribution can change during a game's lifecycle. *Minecraft* is therefore considered as a historical trajectory from a one-person project to a large commercial property, rather than as a claim that it remains an independent production today. ## 3. Comparative interpretation

The cases show that independent development does not imply one design style. They represent distinct paths: player-created content (*Minecraft*), character-centred choice (*Undertale*), procedural variation (*The Binding of Isaac*), connected life-simulation systems (*Stardew Valley*), and a tightly authored world made by a small team (*Hollow Knight*).

For **One More Day**, the most relevant lessons come from *Undertale* (character attachment and meaningful choices) and *Stardew Valley* (integrating setting, routines, relationships, and themes). *Hollow Knight* offers a scope lesson: a coherent world and strong identity matter more than raw size. *The Binding of Isaac* is a useful contrast—procedural replayability can work, but it is not required for a fixed, authored story.

The comparison suggests design hypotheses for *One More Day*: dialogue should fit character relationships, home and hospital spaces should serve distinct narrative functions, and music should reinforce emotional identity. Test these ideas through a playable prototype and player feedback; this desk-based comparison does not prove them.

## 4. Limits of the evidence

This purposive comparison selects five well-known games for contrast, not representativeness. Source depth varies: the strongest game-specific academic evidence here concerns *Minecraft*, *Undertale*, and *Stardew Valley*; the source on *The Binding of Isaac* is a game-studies commentary, while the *Hollow Knight* source is a developer interview. Such sources document mechanics and production history, but are not independent assessments of commercial impact. The evidence does **not** establish that a title caused industry-wide change or that design alone explains commercial success. Stronger impact claims would require market data, contemporary trade reporting, historical comparison, and evidence of influence on subsequent developers.
