# Selected Scholarly Articles on Independent Game Development and Industry Evolution

## 1. Industry Evolution and the Indie–Mainstream Relationship

### 1. Innovation in the British Video Game Industry since 1978

**Author:** Denise Tsang (2021)  
**Journal:** *Business History Review*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007680521000398](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007680521000398)

**Description:** This historical study examines the evolution of the British video game industry from 1978 to 2020, including the rise of independent developers, changes in industry structure, and the role of technological developments and digital distribution. It provides historical evidence for examining how independent development has contributed to broader industry changes.

### 2. Is Every Indie Game Independent? Towards the Concept of Independent Game

**Authors:** Maria B. Garda and Paweł Grabarczyk (2016)  
**Journal:** *Game Studies*  
**Link:** [https://gamestudies.org/1601/articles/garda](https://gamestudies.org/1601/articles/garda)

**Description:** This conceptual study distinguishes independent games from the historically specific category of indie games. It identifies three dimensions of independence: financial, creative, and publishing independence. The article provides a theoretical foundation for defining the subject of research and avoiding overly simplistic classifications of indie games.

### 3. Indie or Mainstream? Tensions and Nuances between the Alternative and the Mainstream in Indie Games

**Author:** Óliver Pérez Latorre (2016)  
**Journal:** *Anàlisi*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.7238/a.v0i54.2818](https://doi.org/10.7238/a.v0i54.2818)

**Description:** This article examines the relationship between independent and mainstream game culture, focusing on the tensions and overlaps between alternative creative practices and commercial production. It is relevant to investigating how indie games relate to the wider industry rather than existing as an entirely separate sector.

### 4. The Indiepocalypse: The Political-Economy of Independent Game Development Labor in Contemporary Indie Markets

**Author:** Nadav D. Lipkin (2019)  
**Journal:** *Game Studies*  
**Link:** [https://gamestudies.org/1902/articles/lipkin](https://gamestudies.org/1902/articles/lipkin)

**Description:** This study examines the economic and labour conditions of independent PC game development, including concerns about market saturation and the sustainability of indie production. It provides a critical perspective on the limitations of creative independence and helps explain why experimentation does not automatically lead to commercial success.

## 2. Game Production, Technology, and Distribution

### 5. From Aggressively Formalised to Intensely In/formalised: Accounting for a Wider Range of Videogame Development Practices

**Author:** Brendan Keogh (2019)  
**Journal:** *Creative Industries Journal*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.1080/17510694.2018.1532760](https://doi.org/10.1080/17510694.2018.1532760)

**Description:** This article examines the relationship between formal professional production and informal game-making practices. It argues for a broader understanding of game development that includes hobbyists, amateurs, students, artists, and independent creators. It helps explain how changes in production practices can expand participation in the video game industry.

### 6. Platformisation in Game Development

**Authors:** Aleena Chia, Brendan Keogh, Dale Leorke, and Benjamin Nicoll (2020)

**Journal:** *Internet Policy Review*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.14763/2020.4.1515](https://doi.org/10.14763/2020.4.1515)

**Description:** This article examines game-making platforms through examples including Unity and Twine. It explores how platforms shape the conditions of game production and influence the opportunities available to developers. It is useful for analysing how development tools and technical infrastructures affect independent game creation.

### 7. Disrupting Video Game Distribution: A Diachronic Affordance Analysis of Steam’s Platformization Strategy

**Author:** Stefan Werning (2019)  
**Journal:** *Nordic Journal of Media Studies*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.2478/njms-2019-0007](https://doi.org/10.2478/njms-2019-0007)

**Description:** This study traces changes in Steam's distribution platform between 2007 and 2018, examining its development into a platform that combines game distribution with social and community features. It provides a framework for understanding how digital distribution platforms reshape relationships between developers, players, and the industry.

### 8. Aspects of Independent Game Production: An Exploratory Study

**Authors:** Leônidas S. Pereira and Maurício M. S. Bernardes (2018)  
**Journal:** *Computers in Entertainment*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.1145/3276322](https://doi.org/10.1145/3276322)

**Description:** Based on semi-structured interviews with lead developers from seven Brazilian independent game companies, this exploratory study examines organisational structures and project management practices. It provides empirical insights into how independent studios organise production compared with larger game companies.

### 9. Itch.io and the One-Dollar-Game: How Distribution Platforms Affect the Ontology of (Games as) a Medium

**Author:** Stefan Werning (2019)  
**Journal:** *On_Culture: The Open Journal for the Study of Culture*  
**Link:** [https://www.on-culture.org/journal/issue-8/one-dollar-game/](https://www.on-culture.org/journal/issue-8/one-dollar-game/)

**Description:** This article investigates how the independent publishing platform itch.io helped shape the one-dollar game as a distinctive category of digital game. Using a comparative content analysis of nearly 300 games, it examines how platform features can enable specific forms of game expression and distribution.

## 3. Economic and Social Conditions of Independent Development

### 10. The Missing Producer: Rethinking Indie Cultural Production in Terms of Entrepreneurship, Relational Labour, and Sustainability

**Authors:** Jennifer R. Whitson, Bart Simon, and Felan Parker (2021)  
**Journal:** *European Journal of Cultural Studies*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.1177/1367549418810082](https://doi.org/10.1177/1367549418810082)

**Description:** Drawing on more than 60 interviews and 120 surveys with indie game developers, this study examines entrepreneurship, professional relationships, and the less visible work required to sustain independent production. It helps explain the organisational and social challenges faced by developers seeking creative autonomy.

### 11. Streaming Ambivalence: Livestreaming and Indie Game Development

**Authors:** Felan Parker and Matthew E. Perks (2021)  
**Journal:** *Convergence*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.1177/13548565211027809](https://doi.org/10.1177/13548565211027809)

**Description:** Based on interviews with independent commercial game developers in Toronto and Montréal, this article examines the potential benefits and risks of livestreaming for game production, promotion, and community-building. It challenges the assumption that streaming necessarily provides visibility or commercial success for indie games.

### 12. “Don’t Clone My Indie Game, Bro”: Informal Cultures of Videogame Regulation in the Independent Sector

**Author:** Tom Phillips (2015)  
**Journal:** *Cultural Trends*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.1080/09548963.2015.1031480](https://doi.org/10.1080/09548963.2015.1031480)

**Description:** This article investigates informal norms surrounding creativity, imitation, and intellectual property within the independent game sector. It is useful for discussing how indie communities regulate the reuse of ideas and how creative influence can produce both innovation and conflict.

### 13. Cultural Production of Video Games: Conditions of Control and Resistance

**Authors:** Sian Tomkinson and Tauel Harper (2022)  
**Journal:** *gamevironments*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.48783/gameviron.v17i17.191](https://doi.org/10.48783/gameviron.v17i17.191)

**Description:** This study examines corporate control over game production and the ways that player communities and digital tools can challenge established power structures. Although it is not limited to indie development, it provides a broader theoretical perspective on participation, control, and alternative forms of cultural production in the game industry.

### 14. Desirable Work: Creative Autonomy and the Everyday Turn in Game Production

**Author:** Chris J. Young (2024; first published online in 2023)  
**Journal:** *New Media & Society*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448221146480](https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448221146480)

**Description:** Based on an ethnographic study of the Toronto game-making community, this article examines how accessible development tools and distribution platforms have changed the experiences and identities of game developers. It explores the relationship between creative autonomy, everyday game-making, and precarious working conditions.

## 4. Independent Game Development as a Creative Process

### 15. Noita: A Long Journey of a Game Idea

**Authors:** Annakaisa Kultima, Riina Ojanen, and Niklas Nylund (2024)  
**Journal:** *Transactions of the Digital Games Research Association*  
**Link:** [https://doi.org/10.26503/todigra.v7i1.2186](https://doi.org/10.26503/todigra.v7i1.2186)

**Description:** This case study reconstructs the development of the independent game *Noita* over more than ten years. The authors analyse development diaries, prototypes, builds, interviews, and player engagement to create a timeline of more than 150 events across seven phases. It demonstrates how the timeline method can be used to investigate the evolution of a game idea and the influences shaping its development.

**Relevance to the Diploma Project**

Together, these studies provide a foundation for examining independent game development as a potential contributor to the evolution of the video game industry. They cover the definition of independence, historical industry changes, technology and distribution platforms, production practices, economic constraints, community norms, and creative experimentation.

The literature should be analysed critically rather than treated as proof that independent development always produces innovation. Historical studies can demonstrate changes over time, conceptual work can clarify what independence means, and empirical studies can document specific production practices. However, claims that indie games have influenced mainstream design or the wider industry require evidence of that relationship rather than assumptions based on a game's originality alone.

The practical project, *One More Day*, can apply selected findings from this research to the design of an original 2D narrative game. It will be presented as an application of the research, not as proof of the independent game sector’s overall impact.
