# Existing System Comparison: Narrative Indie Games

## 1. Purpose

This section compares five existing independent games that use narrative as an important part of the player experience: *To the Moon*, *OneShot*, *Night in the Woods*, *OMORI*, and *Undertale*.

The objective is to identify different approaches to narrative structure, player agency, exploration, character development, audiovisual presentation, and replayability. The comparison will provide a reference for design decisions in *One More Day*, an original 2D narrative game.

The selected games are not assumed to be identical to the proposed project. They represent different approaches to combining storytelling with interactive gameplay.

## 3. Key Findings

The comparison identifies five different approaches to narrative-focused game design.

**To the Moon** demonstrates how emotional storytelling can remain central when combat and complex action mechanics are absent. Its structure is a useful reference for games that prioritize narrative progression over mechanical complexity.

**OneShot** demonstrates how the relationship between the player and the fictional world can become part of the storytelling method. Its unusual puzzle design makes it especially relevant when considering how interaction itself can communicate narrative information.

**Night in the Woods** demonstrates how exploration, everyday activities, and character interactions can support a narrative about personal and social circumstances. It is a useful reference for building a believable setting and revealing information through ordinary interactions.

**OMORI** demonstrates how exploration, psychological themes, contrasting environments, and conventional RPG mechanics can work together within a narrative-focused experience.

**Undertale** demonstrates how player interaction with characters can become a major part of the narrative system. The distinction between violent and nonviolent encounters is especially relevant to studying how mechanics can reinforce a game's themes.

## 4. Implications for One More Day

The comparison suggests several design questions to consider during the development of *One More Day*:

- How should narrative information be delivered: through linear sequences, exploration, dialogue, environmental details, or a combination of these approaches?

- How much should the player's decisions influence the experience and outcome of the story?

- What role should exploration and interaction play beyond moving the narrative forward?

- How can audiovisual choices reinforce the intended atmosphere and emotional tone?

- Is replayability a primary design objective, or should the game prioritize a focused, directed first playthrough?

These questions should inform the project's requirements and later evaluation plan. The final design choices must depend on the actual concept, scope, and intended experience of *One More Day*, rather than simply copying mechanics from existing games.

## 5. Limitations

This comparison is based primarily on publicly available official game descriptions and documented game features. It provides a structured initial comparison but does not constitute a complete empirical evaluation of player experience.

Claims about audience response, comparative emotional impact, commercial influence, or the effectiveness of particular design choices would require further evidence, such as peer-reviewed studies, developer interviews, critical analyses, or documented user research.

The comparison should therefore be treated as a preliminary design analysis. It will be refined after the intended narrative, central gameplay loop, and player experience of *One More Day* are fully specified.
